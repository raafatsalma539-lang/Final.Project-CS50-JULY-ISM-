The Complete Timeline of Palestinian History

Click any event name to see its full details.

1. Ancient Canaanite Palestine

c. 3200 - 1200 BCE

DateEvent
c. 9000 BCE Jericho: the world's oldest inhabited city

Excavations show Jericho in the Jordan Valley has been inhabited since the Neolithic period, with defensive stone walls dating back to around 8000 BCE.
3200-2000 BCE Rise of the Canaanite city-states

Independent Canaanite city-states emerged in Palestine, such as Megiddo, Hazor, and Jerusalem, linked by trade with Egypt and Mesopotamia.
1550-1200 BCE Egyptian control of Canaan

Pharaonic Egypt exercised authority over the cities of Canaan, as documented in the Amarna letters between local rulers and the Egyptian court.

2. Sea Peoples and Ancient Kingdoms

c. 1200 - 539 BCE

DateEvent
1200-1150 BCE The "Sea Peoples" settle the coast, giving Palestine its name

Groups known as the Peleset settled the southern coast and founded Gaza, Ashkelon, and Ashdod. Their name later gave rise to "Palestine."
722 BCE Assyrian conquest of the Kingdom of Israel

The Assyrian Empire overran the Kingdom of Israel, and its capital Samaria fell, with much of its population deported.
586 BCE Babylonian conquest and the exile of Judah

Babylonian king Nebuchadnezzar II destroyed Jerusalem and its Temple, exiling part of the population of Judah to Babylon.

3. Persians, Greeks, and Romans

539 BCE - 636 CE

DateEvent
539 BCE Achaemenid Persian rule

After Babylon fell to Cyrus the Great, Palestine became part of the Persian Empire, and some exiles returned to rebuild the Temple.
332 BCE Alexander the Great's conquest

Alexander the Great swept through the region after defeating the Persian Empire, drawing Palestine into the Hellenistic world.
63 BCE Roman conquest under Pompey

Roman general Pompey entered Jerusalem, bringing the region under Roman authority; Palestine later became a Roman province.
66-135 CE The two Jewish revolts against Rome

The Great Revolt broke out in 66 CE and Rome destroyed the Second Temple in 70 CE. After the Bar Kokhba revolt in 135 CE, Hadrian renamed the province "Syria Palaestina" — the first official use of the name Palestine.

4. The Islamic Conquest and Medieval Rule

636 - 1099 CE

DateEvent
636 CE Battle of Yarmouk and the conquest of the Levant

Muslim armies defeated Byzantine forces at Yarmouk, opening the way for the conquest of the entire Levant, including Palestine.
637 CE Conquest of Jerusalem under Caliph Umar

Jerusalem's patriarch surrendered the city peacefully to Caliph Umar, who granted its Christian residents guaranteed safety.
691 CE Construction of the Dome of the Rock

Umayyad Caliph Abd al-Malik ordered the Dome of the Rock built atop the Haram al-Sharif, one of the oldest Islamic buildings in the world.

5. The Crusades and the Mamluks

1099 - 1516 CE

DateEvent
1099 CE The First Crusade and the fall of Jerusalem

The First Crusade captured Jerusalem and established the Latin Kingdom of Jerusalem.
1187 CE Saladin liberates Jerusalem

Saladin crushed the Crusader army at the Battle of Hattin, then entered Jerusalem, ending nearly ninety years of Crusader rule.
1260 CE The Battle of Ain Jalut

The Mamluks defeated the Mongol army at Ain Jalut in Palestine, halting the Mongol advance and founding Mamluk rule for two centuries.

6. The Ottoman Era

1516 - 1917 CE

DateEvent
1516 CE The Ottoman conquest of the Levant

Sultan Selim I defeated the Mamluks, bringing Palestine under Ottoman rule for the next four centuries.
1882 CE Start of organized Jewish immigration

The first organized waves of Jewish immigration to Palestine began, driven by the rise of Zionism in Europe.
1917 CE Jerusalem falls to British forces

British forces under General Allenby entered Jerusalem during World War I, ending Ottoman rule.

7. The British Mandate

1917 - 1948 CE

DateEvent
Nov 2, 1917 The Balfour Declaration

Britain announced support for a "national home for the Jewish people" in Palestine, without consulting its native inhabitants.
1936-1939 CE The Great Palestinian Revolt

A widespread Palestinian uprising against British rule and Jewish immigration was met with harsh military repression.
Nov 29, 1947 UN Partition Resolution 181

The UN adopted a resolution to partition Palestine into two states. Arab leaders rejected it; Zionist leadership accepted it.

8. The Nakba and the Founding of Israel

1947 - 1949 CE

DateEvent
May 14, 1948 Declaration of the State of Israel

As the British Mandate ended, Ben-Gurion declared the State of Israel, and several Arab armies entered the war the next day.
1947-1949 CE The Nakba: displacement of hundreds of thousands

Around 700,000-750,000 Palestinians fled or were displaced, and hundreds of villages were destroyed. This event is known as the Nakba.
1949 CE Armistice agreements and partition

Israel came to control about 78% of Mandatory Palestine; Egypt administered Gaza, and Jordan annexed the West Bank in 1950.

9. From the Naksa to Oslo

1964 - 1993 CE

DateEvent
1964 CE Founding of the PLO

The Palestine Liberation Organization was founded, later becoming the sole legitimate representative of the Palestinian people.
June 1967 The Six-Day War (the Naksa)

Israel occupied the West Bank, East Jerusalem, and Gaza, along with Sinai and the Golan Heights, in an event known as the Naksa.
Dec 1987 The First Palestinian Intifada

A widespread popular uprising in the occupied territories returned the Palestinian cause to international attention.

10. Oslo and the Second Intifada

1993 - 2005 CE

DateEvent
Sep 13, 1993 Signing of the Oslo Accords

The PLO and Israel signed a Declaration of Principles establishing an interim Palestinian authority in parts of the West Bank and Gaza.
Sep 2000 Outbreak of the Second Intifada

After the Camp David talks failed, a more violent uprising broke out, lasting years with heavy losses on both sides.
2002-2005 CE Separation barrier and Gaza disengagement

Israel began building a separation barrier around parts of the West Bank, then withdrew its settlements from Gaza in 2005.

11. Palestinian Division and the Siege of Gaza

2006 - 2023 CE

DateEvent
Jan 2006 Hamas wins legislative elections

Hamas won a majority of seats in the Palestinian Legislative Council, plunging the Authority into political crisis.
2007 CE The Fatah-Hamas split and the blockade

Hamas took control of Gaza after internal fighting, and Israel imposed a tight blockade lasting more than fifteen years.
2008-2021 CE Repeated rounds of military confrontation

Several major rounds of confrontation took place between Israel and armed factions in Gaza, notably in 2008, 2014, and 2021.

12. Palestine Today

2023 - Present

DateEvent
Oct 7, 2023 The October 7 attack and the Gaza war

Hamas launched a large-scale attack on Israeli communities near Gaza; Israel responded with an extensive military campaign.
2023-2024 CE A wide-scale humanitarian crisis in Gaza

Intense bombardment and ground operations caused widespread destruction and a large civilian death toll, alongside a displacement and famine crisis.
2024-2026 CE Ceasefire and diplomatic efforts

Multiple rounds of mediation, mainly by Qatar, Egypt, and the United States, aimed at truces and hostage exchanges continue as events unfold.
